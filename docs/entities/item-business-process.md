# Item Entity Business Process

[← Back to Item LDM](item.md)

## Purpose and scope

This guide explains how Core Listing Service (CLS) produces and changes the
listing information used by the Item LDM. It uses a small set of
**canonical business events** so users can understand
the data without learning every endpoint or storage document. The canonical
names are documentation labels; the repository event names are retained as
evidence where CLS publishes them.

CLS records accepted listing state. It does not by itself decide final buyer
eligibility, checkout price, search or View Item rendering, policy outcomes,
delivery promises, orders, refunds, or payments. The Item LDM can also combine
CLS state with catalog, classification, warehouse, search, and other
downstream sources.

## Event overview

| Canonical business event | Action | Business description | Representative CLS evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Listing created** | Produce | A seller or authorized workflow submits a new listing and CLS accepts its identity, seller context, and initial listing information. | Core item/listing create flows |
| **Listing revised** | Update | A seller or authorized workflow changes listing content or configuration, such as title, category, condition, media, shipping, payment, tax, returns, or policies. | `ITEM.CATEGORY.UPDATED`; item/listing update flows |
| **Offer or availability changed** | Update | Price, quantity, auction terms, offers, promotions, or inventory availability changes. | `ITEM.AVL.UPDATED`, `ITEM.LOCK.EXPIRED` |
| **Listing lifecycle changed** | Update | A listing is scheduled, activated, renewed, ended, finalized, or relisted. | `LISTING.RENEW`, `LISTING.END` |
| **Listing control changed** | Update | A hold, restriction, administrative action, safety control, or privacy-related control changes the accepted state. | `ITEM.HOLDSTATUS.UPDATED`; administrative and PII operations |
| **Listing reconciled** | Operational signal | A migration, dual-write, repair, or consistency process asks other systems to reconcile listing state. | `LISTING.RECONCILE`, `ITEM.DUALWRITE.FAILED` |
| **Listing data removed or privacy-cleared** | Clear/remove | Supported personal information is cleared or anonymized, or a supported entity is removed. Privacy clearing is not the same as ending a listing. | PII removal and deletion processors |
| **Draft changed** | Produce/update/remove | A seller creates, revises, reconciles, or deletes working draft information. A draft is not a live listing. | `DRAFT.CREATE`, `DRAFT.UPDATE`, draft dual-write failure signals |
| **Derived or exposed state** | Derived/expose | CLS exposes current state and revision history; downstream systems may derive, standardize, or enrich additional Item LDM values. | Current-state reads, revision-history reads, downstream Item LDM sources |

An event is not necessarily a complete listing snapshot. Some events are facts
about a state change, some tell consumers to re-read current state, and some
are repair or failure signals.

## Canonical business events

### Listing created

When a seller or authorized eBay workflow submits a listing, CLS validates and
records the listing identity, marketplace and seller context, application or
channel information, and initial seller-provided content. Consumers can use
this event category to understand when listing state was first accepted, but
creation alone does not prove that the listing is live, buyer-visible, or
successfully sold.

### Listing revised

When a seller or authorized workflow changes what is being offered or how it is
presented, CLS accepts and records the revised content or configuration,
including categories, condition, media, shipping, payment, tax, returns, and
other listing settings. Consumers can use this category to understand the
latest seller-configured listing state, while standardized or enriched values
may be supplied by other processes.

### Offer or availability changed

When an inventory or listing workflow changes price, quantity, auction or
offer terms, promotions, or availability, CLS records the accepted commercial
state and may publish an availability signal. Consumers should treat an
availability signal as a prompt to refresh the listing state, not as a
complete price or quantity snapshot; listing-currency price is not the final
buyer price or checkout total.

### Listing lifecycle changed

When a seller, scheduler, or authorized eBay operation schedules, activates,
renews, ends, finalizes, or relists a listing, CLS records the resulting
status, dates, duration, end reason, auction outcome, or relist relationship.
Consumers can use this category to understand the lifecycle state recorded by
CLS, but lifecycle state alone does not establish search visibility, purchase
eligibility, checkout availability, or order outcomes.

### Listing control changed

When eBay operations or a safety, administrative, or privacy workflow places a
hold, applies a restriction, ends or reinstates a listing administratively, or
changes a supported control, CLS records the resulting state. Consumers can
use this category to identify controls recorded by CLS, but one control value
does not represent the complete policy, fraud, visibility, or buyer-eligibility
decision.

### Listing reconciled

When migration, dual-write, repair, or consistency work detects that listing
state may need correction, CLS can publish a reconciliation or failure signal
for other systems. Consumers can use this category to identify operational
coordination, but it should not be interpreted as a seller revision or as
proof that every downstream copy has already been corrected.

### Listing data removed or privacy-cleared

When a supported privacy or deletion operation removes personal information,
CLS clears or anonymizes the supported values and may remove supported entity
state. Consumers must distinguish privacy clearing from listing ending and
from physical deletion; the resulting record may continue to exist with
specific values removed.

### Draft changed

When a seller saves, revises, reconciles, or deletes a draft, CLS records the
seller's working state and maintains draft associations where supported.
Consumers can use this category to understand saved seller work, but a draft
does not prove publication, buyer visibility, or purchase eligibility.

### Derived or exposed state

After CLS accepts a business event, consumers can retrieve the latest recorded
listing state and, where supported, revision history. The Item LDM may also
derive or enrich values from catalog, classification, warehouse, search, or
dimensional sources. These values should be interpreted using their field
definitions and source lineage rather than assumed to be direct CLS writes.

## Consumer contract

Consumers can use the Item LDM to understand listing information accepted by
CLS, the recorded commercial and lifecycle state, seller working drafts where
applicable, and changes or signals that CLS records or publishes. They must
combine it with other data before concluding what a buyer will pay, whether a
buyer can complete checkout, how policy or fraud rules were applied, what View
Item will display, how an order or payment completed, or what delivery outcome
occurred.

## Source and repository boundary

The Item LDM documentation is broader than the CLS source model. Some fields
are derived, standardized, historical, dimensional, financial, or search
values from sources other than CLS. The mapping marks those fields as
**Derived or exposed** rather than attributing them to a CLS event without
evidence.

During migration between legacy and current listing paths, the same business
change can reach different consumers at different times. Current state,
revision history, event signals, and downstream LDM values may therefore have
different availability timing.
