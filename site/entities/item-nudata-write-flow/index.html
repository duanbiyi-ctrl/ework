    NuData write-flow research

    Scope and answer

    This is a repository-scoped walkthrough of crlstngsv. In this document, “NuDoc” means the NuData/MonStor document classes and docsets represented by this repository. The repository does not use SQL DDL for these stores; it builds JSON documents and sends MonStor RequestFactory insert, upsert, update, and delete mutations.

    There are 22 declared logical docsets in the source:

    Keyspace family Domain purpose Logical docsets Count
    ItemKeyspace Current-state identity and operational data for one listing item or variation __ROOT__, ItemCompatibility, ItemDescription, ItemQtyPrice, ItemDetails, BusinessPolicies, UuidMap, ItemLock 8
    ItemGroupKeyspace Shared current-state data for an MSKU item group __ROOT__, GroupDescription, GroupBusinessPolicies 3
    ListingDraft Working content and session state before or during listing publication __ROOT__, ItemDraft, ItemGroupDraft, ItemDraftCompatibility, ItemGroupDraftCompatibility, ItemGroupDraftDesc 6
    UserListingDraft User-scoped index of active listing drafts UserItemDraftMap 1
    ListingRevisionKeyspace Historical listing and variation changes, separate from current state __ROOT__, ListingRevision, ListingRevisionDetail, VariationRevisionDetail 4
    Total 22

    For ordinary live data writes, the item create/replace family writes six docsets, item-group create/replace writes three, an SSKU draft writes two or three documents and an MSKU draft writes three plus one ItemDraft per variation, or four plus one when group compatibility is present, active user-draft indexing writes one, and a revision insert writes one to three document types. UuidMap and ItemLock are auxiliary item docsets. Archive hold/version fallback writes the root docset in an archive keyspace. Therefore, “22 tables” is the schema declaration count, not the number of documents written by every request.

    If a keyspace-qualified sink is counted only when a write is proven in code, there are 24 sink pairs:

    • 8 in live ItemKeyspace;
    • 3 in live ItemGroupKeyspace;
    • 6 in ListingDraft;
    • 1 in UserListingDraft;
    • 4 in ListingRevisionKeyspace;
    • 1 archive pair, ItemKeyspaceArchive/__ROOT__;
    • 1 archive pair, ItemGroupKeyspaceArchive/__ROOT__.

    The archive keyspaces reuse the item and group schema classes, so they add two physical keyspace/docset sinks but no new logical docset class. The count does not claim that all 24 sinks are touched by one business operation.

    The five rows above are the logical live keyspaces described by this document. ItemKeyspaceArchive and ItemGroupKeyspaceArchive are archive variants that reuse the root schemas; they are physical write targets, not additional logical keyspace families.

    Keyspace and docset purpose catalog

    This catalog explains what each keyspace represents and why each docset exists. The hierarchy role describes the document's relationship to its ancestor; it does not imply that every docset is written by every operation.

    ItemKeyspace

    ItemKeyspace is the current-state family for one individual listing item or one variation of an MSKU listing. The item root is the identity anchor. Compatibility, description, quantity/price, details, and business-policy payloads are separated so they can be read and updated independently. UuidMap and ItemLock are auxiliary operational trees in the same keyspace, not additional parts of the normal six-docset item create/replace payload.

    Docset Description and purpose Hierarchy and key context
    __ROOT__ (ItemRoot) Broad current-state item document: listing identity, seller/location, lifecycle, category, policy references, flags, identifiers, media references, and other item-level state. Root of the item ancestor tree; persisted primary key PK, normally equal to itemId.
    ItemCompatibility Fitment and compatibility payload for the item, with expanded or compressed representation. Child of the item ancestor; ItemCompID, normally COMPAT_<itemId>.
    ItemDescription Seller/item description payload and its revision timestamp, with expanded or compressed representation. Child of the item ancestor; ItemDescID, normally DESC_<itemId>.
    ItemQtyPrice Commercial and inventory state: available quantity, sold quantity, remnant quantity, and price variants. Child of the item ancestor; ItemQtyPriceID, normally QTY_PRICE_<itemId>.
    ItemDetails Additional item-level details such as high-precision prices, relaxed rules, source listing, and supporting documents. Child of the item ancestor; itemDetailsID, normally ITEM_DETAILS_<itemId>.
    BusinessPolicies Item-level return, payment, and shipping policy content and identifiers. Child of the item ancestor; plcsId, normally BIZ_POL_<itemId>.
    UuidMap (ItemUUIDMapping) Auxiliary seller-and-UUID lookup that resolves listingId and listingAppId for UUID-specific operations. Separate UUID ancestor tree in the same keyspace; composite key is seller ID plus UUID, with UUID_MAP child semantics.
    ItemLock Inventory reservation lifecycle state for active, committed, cancelled, and extended locks, including the aggregate summary lock. Auxiliary child under the item ancestor; persisted key pk, normally LOCK_<lockId>; summary key is LOCK_$$$SUMMARY$$$.

    ItemGroupKeyspace

    ItemGroupKeyspace stores shared current-state data for an MSKU group. It identifies group membership and variation context while keeping variation-specific price, quantity, lifecycle, and other sellable state in the individual ItemKeyspace families. The group root references member item IDs; this is a logical relationship across keyspaces, not a cross-keyspace parent-child key.

    Docset Description and purpose Hierarchy and key context
    __ROOT__ (ItemGroupRoot) Group identity, member item IDs, shared title/images, variation traits, group identifiers, version, and group lifecycle data. Root of the group ancestor tree; persisted primary key PK, normally equal to groupId.
    GroupDescription Shared description for the group, optionally compressed, with its revision timestamp. Child of the group ancestor; groupDescId, normally GROUP_DESC_<groupId>.
    GroupBusinessPolicies Shared group-level return, payment, and shipping policy content. Child of the group ancestor; plcsId, normally BIZ_POL_<groupId>. It inherits the policy field shape but has a distinct collection.

    ListingDraft

    ListingDraft is the working-state family used while a listing is being constructed or revised before publication. A single draftId ancestor can contain SSKU item content, MSKU group content, compatibility content, group description content, and one ItemDraft per active MSKU variation. These docsets are storage children of the same draft ancestor even when one docset is logically associated with the item or group portion.

    Docset Description and purpose Hierarchy and key context
    __ROOT__ (ListingDraftRoot) Draft session state, flags, scratch values, progress metadata, dates, and the list of variation item IDs. Root of the draft ancestor tree; persisted primary key PK, normally equal to draftId.
    ItemDraft Draft content for an SSKU item or one MSKU variation, including title, description, price, quantity, seller, terms, categories, media, and revision metadata. Direct child of the draft ancestor; PK, normally ITEM_<draftId> for SSKU or ITEM_<draftId>_<variationId> for an MSKU variation.
    ItemDraftCompatibility Optional SSKU compatibility payload for the draft item. Direct storage child of the draft ancestor, logically associated with ItemDraft; DraftCompID, normally COMPAT_<draftId>.
    ItemGroupDraft Shared MSKU draft-group content: group title, images, variation traits, seller group key, policies, and group ID. Direct child of the draft ancestor; PK, normally GROUP_<draftId>.
    ItemGroupDraftDesc Optional shared MSKU group description and description revision metadata. Direct storage child of the draft ancestor, logically associated with ItemGroupDraft; PK, normally GROUPDESC_<draftId>.
    ItemGroupDraftCompatibility Optional MSKU group-level compatibility payload. Direct storage child of the draft ancestor, logically associated with ItemGroupDraft; DraftCompID, normally GROUPCOMPAT_<draftId>.

    UserListingDraft

    UserListingDraft is an index family for finding active listing drafts by user. It stores summary and lookup metadata rather than the full draft payload. It is intentionally separate from ListingDraft, so a user-index mutation does not automatically mutate the draft content tree.

    Docset Description and purpose Hierarchy and key context
    UserItemDraftMap (UserListingDraftMap) User-scoped summary of an active draft, including draftId, user, title, marketplace/application, category, images, creation mode, and timestamps. User-and-draft composite ancestor key; the schema class has no persisted primary-key field. Conceptually UserListingDraft/<userId>/<draftId>.

    ListingRevisionKeyspace

    ListingRevisionKeyspace stores historical changes rather than the latest listing state. A listing ancestor can have an optional retention root, one or more generated revision documents, and detail children describing the non-variation and variation properties changed by each revision.

    Docset Description and purpose Hierarchy and key context
    __ROOT__ (ListingRevisionRoot) Conditional retention state for a listing's revision history, including the listing identifier and TTL-maintenance fields. Optional sibling under the listing ancestor; lstgId identifies the listing. Normal revision inserts do not create it.
    ListingRevision Revision summary: changed property groups, revision/version identifiers, application, significant revision count, creation flag, and revision timestamp. Generated child of the listing ancestor; the schema has no persisted key field.
    ListingRevisionDetail Non-variation property-level change record with property group, property, change type, old value, new value, and display label. Generated child of a particular ListingRevision; the schema has no persisted key field.
    VariationRevisionDetail Variation-specific property-level change record with variation ID, changed variation property, trait values, change type, and old/new values. Generated child of a particular ListingRevision; the schema has no persisted key field.

    1. End-to-end producer flow

    The common path is:

    REST resource or V1 dual-write trigger
  -> validation and processor selection
  -> V2 model-to-NuData document converter
  -> ancestor/child key construction
  -> transactional MonStor mutation
  -> later multi-docset get/query response

    The V2 resources call processors directly. V1 listing and draft resources first pass through DualWriteProcessor or DraftDualWriteProcessor; those processors convert legacy payloads to V2 models and decide whether the NuData write is asynchronous, synchronous, parallel, or disabled by a ramp/config gate. A successful V2 write is not evidence that Oracle/Caty was or was not also written; those systems are outside this document's count.

    The persistence boundary is a set of documents under an item, group, draft, user, or listing-revision ancestor. DocumentConverter, ItemGroupDocumentConverter, and DraftDocumentConverter construct the content. The processors then use MonStor InsertRequest, BatchMutateRequest, UpdateRequest, or DeleteRequest, generally with transactional options for multi-document operations. Consumers read current item and group state with multi-docset gets, read drafts through draft queries/gets, read active drafts through UserItemDraftMap, and read revision history through ancestor queries.

    1.1. From request input to Java entity

    There are two main producer entry paths:

    1. Direct V2 client input. A caller sends a JSON request to a V2 resource. The web framework binds the request body to the Java parameter declared by the resource: Item, ItemGroup, PriceAndQuantity, ListingDraft, or another operation-specific request type. The resource performs authorization and passes that typed object to the corresponding processor. For example, CoreItemResource.createItem(Item) passes an Item to CoreItemProcessor.insertItem(Item, ...), while CoreItemGroupResource.createItemGroup(ItemGroup) passes an ItemGroup to CoreItemGroupProcessor.insertItemGroup(ItemGroup, ...).

    2. Legacy V1 dual-write input. A legacy listing request is first represented by V1 Java entities such as Listing, ListingDetails, and SaleBo. DualWriteProcessor invokes ListingConverter, which builds one or more V2 Item objects and, only for an MSKU listing, an ItemGroup. The V1 listing ID, variation data, seller SKU, pricing, quantity, lifecycle, policies, and other source values are normalized into the V2 model before any NuData document is built. Runtime ramps can make this conversion/write synchronous, asynchronous, parallel, or disabled.

    The important boundary is that NuData writers do not normally serialize the incoming HTTP JSON directly into storage. The request is first represented as a Java domain/request object, then passed through the processor and converter logic that applies the path's validation and normalization. The Java object is the source of truth for the document-building step described below.

    1.2. Item producer path

    For a direct V2 item create or replace, the flow is:

    JSON request body
  -> Java Item
  -> CoreItemProcessor.insertItem/upsertItem
  -> DocumentConverter
  -> six ItemKeyspace Documents
  -> ancestor/child Keys
  -> transactional MonStor InsertRequest

    CoreItemProcessor derives stable child identifiers from itemId, such as COMPAT_<itemId>, DESC_<itemId>, QTY_PRICE_<itemId>, ITEM_DETAILS_<itemId>, and BIZ_POL_<itemId>. It then asks DocumentConverter to build:

    Java source object Converter method NuData document
    Item getRoot(item) ItemKeyspace/__ROOT__
    Item getItemCompatibility(item, compatibilityId) ItemKeyspace/ItemCompatibility
    Item getDescription(item, descriptionId) ItemKeyspace/ItemDescription
    Item getQtyPrice(item, qtyPriceId) ItemKeyspace/ItemQtyPrice
    Item getItemDetails(item, itemDetailsId) ItemKeyspace/ItemDetails
    Item.businessPolicies getBusinessPolicies(..., bizPolId) ItemKeyspace/BusinessPolicies

    Each converter creates a NuData schema object, copies or transforms Java properties, serializes that object through the configured Jackson ObjectMapper into an ObjectNode, and wraps it with DocumentFactory.createDocument(...). Examples of transformations include category and lifecycle construction, image conversion, amount conversion, expanded-versus-compressed descriptions and compatibilities, and conversion of return/payment/shipping terms.

    The processor then builds the item ancestor key and child keys, adds the six documents to a transactional MonStor request, and executes it. A create path uses addForInsert; a replace/upsert path uses addForUpsert and can apply a core-listing-version filter to prevent an older update from overwriting newer state. A successful response means the accepted current item state has been written to the six logical docsets; it does not mean every possible auxiliary docset such as UuidMap or ItemLock was written.

    1.3. ItemGroup producer path

    For a direct V2 item-group create or the processor's upsert path, the flow is:

    JSON request body
  -> Java ItemGroup
  -> CoreItemGroupProcessor.insertItemGroup/upsertItemGroup
  -> ItemGroupDocumentConverter + DocumentConverter
  -> three ItemGroupKeyspace Documents
  -> ancestor/child Keys
  -> transactional MonStor InsertRequest

    CoreItemGroupProcessor derives GROUP_DESC_<groupId> and BIZ_POL_<groupId>, then builds:

    Java source object Converter method NuData document
    ItemGroup ItemGroupDocumentConverter.getRoot(group, lastModifiedDate) ItemGroupKeyspace/__ROOT__
    ItemGroup ItemGroupDocumentConverter.getDescription(group, descriptionId) ItemGroupKeyspace/GroupDescription
    ItemGroup.businessPolicies DocumentConverter.getBusinessPolicies(..., bizPolId) ItemGroupKeyspace/GroupBusinessPolicies

    The group root contains group identity, member variation IDs, shared images, variation traits, group version, and group-level identifiers. The description and policy documents are separate child documents so they can be updated independently. The group processor adds all three documents to one transactional request; it does not write variation price or quantity into the group root.

    The current CoreItemGroupResource replace methods are stubs that return null; the processor upsert path is nevertheless used by the V1 dual-write flow and is the code-proven group replacement implementation described here.

    1.4. SSKU/MSKU conversion and write branching

    The legacy conversion path determines whether a group exists before the NuData write:

    V1 Listing + SaleBo
  -> ListingConverter.getItemsFromListing(...)
       -> one Item for SSKU
       -> one Item per active variation for MSKU
  -> ListingConverter.getItemGroupFromListing(...)
       -> null for SSKU
       -> one ItemGroup for MSKU
  -> group write (MSKU only)
  -> item write or batch item write

    For an MSKU listing, ListingConverter creates the group ID from the parent listing ID, collects the active variation item IDs into ItemGroup.itemIds, and assigns that parent ID to each variation Item.groupId. Shared description, business policies, compatibilities, and some identifiers are stored on the group and cleared from the variation Item objects instead of being repeated. CoreItemProcessor.insertItems(...) then builds and inserts the complete six-document item family for each variation.

    For an SSKU listing, the converter returns one ordinary Item and no ItemGroup; the processor writes only the item family. An MSKU with one active variation still takes the MSKU branch and writes an ItemGroup whose itemIds contains one variation ID.

    1.5. Draft input follows the same boundary with a draft model

    For V2 listing drafts, the request body is bound to ListingDraft. The draft processor validates it and DraftDocumentConverter builds the draft root, item drafts, optional group draft, descriptions, and compatibility documents. The converter assigns or normalizes draft variation IDs, serializes each NuData schema object into a Document, and the processor writes the assembled documents transactionally. SSKU drafts have item content without a group draft; MSKU drafts have group content plus one item draft per variation.

    1.6. Partial updates do not rebuild the whole Java entity

    Operation-specific requests such as price/quantity, category, product reference, hold status, business-policy reference, description, or item lock updates can bypass the full create/replace conversion. The processor converts only the changed request data or builds an update operation directly against the affected root or child docset. For example, price/quantity changes target ItemQtyPrice and related root state, a category change targets the root category fields, and a lock operation writes ItemLock plus its inventory side effect. Therefore, “one item write” may mean a six-docset replacement or a narrower mutation of one or two existing docsets.

    2. Schema, key layout, field inventory, and model lineage

    Docset is the repository's schema abstraction: a Java class represents a NuData collection and getCollectionName() returns the collection name (crlstngsvService/src/main/java/com/ebay/app/cls/nudata/Docset.java). Nested helper classes such as Price, Lifecycle, TermsAndPolicies, and ShippingTerms are fields inside a document; they are not separate docsets.

    Item family: ItemKeyspace

    The root key is KeyFactory.create(itemId). The child documents are descendants of that root:

    itemId
├── ItemCompatibility / COMPAT_<itemId>
├── ItemDescription / DESC_<itemId>
├── ItemQtyPrice / QTY_PRICE_<itemId>
├── BusinessPolicies / BIZ_POL_<itemId>
├── ItemDetails / ITEM_DETAILS_<itemId>
└── ItemLock / LOCK_<lockId>

    The lock-summary document uses the same ItemLock schema and reserved identifier LOCK_$$$SUMMARY$$$. Lock mutations also update ItemQtyPrice to reflect reserved inventory. UuidMap uses a different key shape: UUID_<sellerId>/<uuid> with a UUID_MAP child.

    Docset Document role and representative fields
    __ROOT__ Core listing state: PK, title, category, lifecycle, seller/listing identifiers, location, images, policies/terms references, version/revision fields, onHold, endRsn, and other item-level state. DocumentConverter.getRoot() maps the V2 Item into this document.
    ItemCompatibility Fitment/compatibility payload: ItemCompID, compat or compressed cmpCompat, and ftExempt.
    ItemDescription Description payload: ItemDescID, desc or compressed compDesc, and descRevsnDt.
    ItemQtyPrice Inventory and commercial values: availQty, qtySold, price, and minRmntSet; price contains fixed, auction, marketing, original-retail, cost-of-goods, and price-guarantee data.
    ItemDetails Additional item details such as hasRlxdRls, high-precision fixed/start prices, source listing, and supporting documents.
    BusinessPolicies Policy content: policy identifiers plus return, payment, and shipping terms.
    UuidMap UUID/seller lookup value containing listingId and listingAppId; used to resolve or anonymize a listing mapping.
    ItemLock Reservation/lock documents with pk, status (A, C, X), quantity, expiration, timestamps, and UUID; the summary record stores aggregate reserved quantity.

    The six-document item create/replace set is: __ROOT__, ItemCompatibility, ItemDescription, ItemQtyPrice, ItemDetails, and BusinessPolicies. The UuidMap and ItemLock documents are not part of the normal item payload mutation.

    ItemKeyspace elaboration

    ItemKeyspace is the current-state store for one individual listing item. __ROOT__ holds the broad item state, while the child docsets isolate compatibility, description, quantity/price, details, and business-policy content that can be updated independently. UuidMap and ItemLock support lookup and reservation workflows rather than representing additional top-level item payloads. Consumers should use the item root as the identity anchor and treat child documents as parts of the same item state, not as independent listings.

    Relationship between ItemKeyspace and ItemGroupKeyspace

    These are separate keyspace families with separate ancestors and separate root documents. ItemKeyspace stores the individual item or variation records; ItemGroupKeyspace stores the multi-variation group identity, membership, shared traits, group description, and group-level policies. For an MSKU group, the group root's itemIds identifies its member item roots, and the item root's grpId points back to the group. This is a logical relationship, not a parent-child key relationship across keyspaces.

    The two families therefore have different write responsibilities: item operations write variation/listing state such as quantity, price, lifecycle, description, and item compatibility, while group operations write membership, variation traits, group description, and group policies. A consumer must read both families to reconstruct a complete MSKU listing; a group document alone does not contain the full variation-level commercial state, and an item document alone does not establish the complete group membership.

    Single-SKU versus one-variation MSKU

    The repository distinguishes SSKU versus MSKU by the listing's MSKU flag, not by counting variation records:

    Listing condition ItemKeyspace result ItemGroupKeyspace result
    SSKU (saleBo.isMsku() is false) One ordinary item document family is created or updated. No ItemGroup record is created; getItemGroupFromListing(...) returns null.
    MSKU with one active/non-deleted variation One item record is created for that variation. Its groupId points to the parent listing/group ID. The MSKU group is still created with __ROOT__, GroupDescription, and GroupBusinessPolicies; the group root's itemIds contains exactly that one variation item ID.
    MSKU with multiple active/non-deleted variations One item record is created per active variation. The same group docsets are created, and itemIds contains all active variation item IDs.

    For the one-variation MSKU case, the group record is therefore not omitted or collapsed into the item record. The group ID is the parent listing ID, while the sole itemIds value is the separate variation item ID. A consumer should not infer “SSKU” merely because itemIds has length one; it must use the listing's MSKU classification and the presence of the group record.

    Item-group family: ItemGroupKeyspace

    The group root is KeyFactory.create(groupId). Child keys are descendants:

    groupId
├── GroupDescription / GROUP_DESC_<groupId>
└── GroupBusinessPolicies / BIZ_POL_<groupId>
    Docset Document role and representative fields
    __ROOT__ Group identity and membership: PK, title, itemIds, seller/group key, variation traits, images, group version, group draft ID, and administrative/lifecycle fields.
    GroupDescription Group description, optionally compressed, with groupDescId and grpDescRevsnDt.
    GroupBusinessPolicies Group-level business policies. It extends the business-policy model but returns the group-specific collection name.

    CoreItemGroupProcessor creates or upserts all three documents transactionally. Group shipping updates touch the root and GroupBusinessPolicies; description updates touch the root and GroupDescription.

    ItemGroupKeyspace elaboration

    ItemGroupKeyspace is the current-state store for an item group, most notably the shared structure of an MSKU listing. Its root describes the group and its member IDs; GroupDescription and GroupBusinessPolicies hold independently addressable group content. GroupBusinessPolicies uses the group collection name even though its fields inherit the item policy schema, so it must not be counted as the item BusinessPolicies docset. Group-level state also does not replace the item-level documents for the group's variations.

    Listing-draft family: ListingDraft

    The draft root is keyed by draftId. The processor uses prefixes such as GROUP_, GROUPDESC_, GROUPCOMPAT_, ITEM_, and COMPAT_. The key relationship is:

    draftId
├── ItemDraft / ITEM_<draftId>                 (SSKU)
├── ItemDraftCompatibility / COMPAT_<draftId>  (optional SSKU)
├── ItemGroupDraft / GROUP_<draftId>            (MSKU)
├── ItemGroupDraftDesc / GROUPDESC_<draftId>   (MSKU)
├── ItemGroupDraftCompatibility / GROUPCOMPAT_<draftId> (optional MSKU)
└── ItemDraft / ITEM_<draftId>_<variationId>   (MSKU variations)
    Docset Document role and representative fields
    __ROOT__ Draft session, flags, scratch details, draft/creation/revision dates, and the list of MSKU variation IDs.
    ItemDraft SSKU or variation content including title, description, price, quantity, seller, listing terms, categories, images, and revision fields.
    ItemGroupDraft MSKU group title, images, variation traits, seller group key, business policies, and group ID.
    ItemDraftCompatibility Optional SSKU compatibility payload, compressed when configured.
    ItemGroupDraftCompatibility Optional MSKU group compatibility payload, compressed when configured.
    ItemGroupDraftDesc MSKU group description, optionally compressed, plus description revision date.

    An SSKU draft normally writes two or three docsets: root, item, and optional item compatibility. An MSKU draft writes root, group, group description, optional group compatibility, and one or more ItemDraft variation documents. The variation count changes the number of documents, not the number of logical docsets.

    ListingDraft elaboration

    ListingDraft is a working-state store for listing construction before or during publication. SSKU drafts use item documents, while MSKU drafts add a group root, group description, optional group compatibility, and one item document per variation. Draft documents are not proof that a live ItemKeyspace or ItemGroupKeyspace listing exists; they are a separate content model and lifecycle.

    User active-draft family: UserListingDraft

    UserItemDraftMap is keyed below a user root and draft identifier. Its representative fields are draftId, marketplace/application identifiers, category, title, image URLs, creation mode/date, user ID, and lastModifiedDate. It is a separate index/map from the content in ListingDraft; updating or deleting one does not imply that the other is automatically changed unless the caller invokes both flows.

    UserListingDraft elaboration

    UserListingDraft is an index for finding active drafts by user. Its UserItemDraftMap document contains summary metadata and lookup identifiers, not the complete draft payload. It can therefore be created, updated, or deleted independently of the corresponding ListingDraft content, subject to the caller's dual-write behavior.

    Revision-history family: ListingRevisionKeyspace

    The listing ID is the ancestor. A normal insert creates a generated child key for ListingRevision, then generated child keys for optional details:

    listingId
├── __ROOT__ / listingId [optional TTL-maintenance root]
└── ListingRevision / generated-guid
    ├── ListingRevisionDetail / generated-guid (zero or more)
    └── VariationRevisionDetail / generated-guid (zero or more)

    The __ROOT__ document is an optional sibling under the listing ancestor. It is created or updated by expired-date maintenance, not by the normal revision insert. ListingRevision contains revised property groups, version/application identifiers, significant revision count, revised date, and updated core-listing version. Detail documents contain revised group/property, change type, old/new values, and (for variation details) variation traits/ID.

    The declared revision root class exists as a schema class and is queried by the revision-history code, but RevisionHistoryNudataClient.addForInsert() explicitly does not create a root document in the normal insert path. A separate expired-date upsert path can create/update that root, so it remains a declared and conditional write target rather than a normal revision-insert target.

    ListingRevisionKeyspace elaboration

    ListingRevisionKeyspace is historical rather than current-state storage. ListingRevision records a revision summary under a listing ancestor, and detail children record property-level changes, including variation-specific changes. A revision document should be interpreted as an event/history record, not as a replacement for the latest ItemKeyspace or ItemGroupKeyspace state. The declared revision root is an exception: it participates in expired-date maintenance but is intentionally omitted from ordinary revision inserts.

    Detailed field schema and source-model lineage

    The following inventory lists the JSON properties declared directly on each docset class. Java types are included where they clarify whether a field is a scalar, list, enum, or embedded object. Fields inside embedded objects are not repeated as docsets: for example, ItemKeyspace/__ROOT__.category is a Category value containing category fields, but Category is not another NuData collection.     Complete NuDoc hierarchies and primary keys

    NuData uses several independent ancestor trees. A keyspace is a storage namespace that can contain more than one tree; ItemKeyspace, for example, contains the normal item tree, the UUID lookup tree, and the item-lock subtree. A docset is a child of another docset only when its storage key is constructed beneath that ancestor. Sharing a keyspace does not by itself make two docsets parent and child.

    Field-schema interpretation

    • The lists above are top-level persisted properties from the repository's Java schema classes. They are not a promise that every property is present on every document; converters may omit null values, and update paths may set or unset only a subset.
    • compat/cmpCompat and desc/compDesc show the repository's expanded versus compressed representation choice.
    • BusinessPolicies is reused by item, group, and draft models as an embedded object, while GroupBusinessPolicies is the separate group docset that inherits the same four top-level policy fields.
    • The four classes named __ROOT__ are different schemas in different packages. Their matching class names do not make them one shared collection across keyspaces.
    • getCollectionName() is the authoritative logical collection name in the source. The revision classes return their fully qualified Java class name, while the revision constants and mutation code use the short docset names ListingRevision, ListingRevisionDetail, and VariationRevisionDetail.

    Write-side lineage notes

    The mappings in these tables follow the write-side converters, not just matching names by convention. A derived mapping means the JSON field is assembled from multiple model properties or converted to a NuData-specific embedded type. An update-only mapping is not populated by the standard create/replace converter; it is changed by a separate processor mutation.

    The item root also has direct update methods that bypass full model conversion. For example, category updates set the nested category fields, price/quantity updates set selected root and child fields, and media updates set images/prdGallryFallbckURL-related root state. Therefore, the tables describe the full-document source model while partial operations may update only a subset.

    The group root and child documents also have partial update paths that can change selected fields without reconstructing the entire ItemGroup payload.

    3. Operation-to-docset write matrix

    The matrix describes docset families, not individual documents. “Optional” means the request shape or operation determines whether the mutation is included.

    Business operation NuData mutation and docsets
    Create/replace V2 item Insert or upsert six item docsets: __ROOT__, ItemCompatibility, ItemDescription, ItemQtyPrice, ItemDetails, BusinessPolicies. Reconcile variants can omit ItemDetails or gate compatibility updates.
    Bulk replace items Calls the same item upsert path independently/asynchronously per item, so the same six-docset family is possible for each item.
    Item compatibility update Upsert ItemCompatibility only.
    Item description/revision update Update item __ROOT__ revision/version fields and ItemDescription.
    Item price/quantity/inventory update Update ItemQtyPrice and usually __ROOT__; may also update BusinessPolicies for payment fields and upsert ItemDetails for high-precision/detail state.
    Quantity/lifecycle inventory update Update ItemQtyPrice and __ROOT__ for quantities, scheduled/end dates, end reason, and core-listing version.
    Shipping/policy update Update item __ROOT__ and BusinessPolicies; group equivalent updates touch group __ROOT__ and GroupBusinessPolicies.
    Category/media/version/administrative item update Usually update item __ROOT__; category, media, hold, duplicate, relist, HTTPS, and related fields are root fields. MSKU variants use batch root updates.
    Item lock create/reactivate/commit/cancel/extend Write/update/delete ItemLock, plus update the item ItemQtyPrice quantities and the ItemLock summary. A lock operation therefore can touch two logical docsets, with ItemQtyPrice as the inventory side effect.
    UUID mapping create/delete/anonymize Insert/delete UuidMap. PII deletion anonymizes the UUID mapping and clears supported PII fields in item root/business-policy documents.
    Delete item variations Delete item root, compatibility, description, quantity/price, business-policy, details, and lock-summary keys. Individual lock children are handled by lock lifecycle operations; this delete list is not a blanket delete of every descendant.
    Create/replace item group Insert or upsert group __ROOT__, GroupDescription, and GroupBusinessPolicies.
    Delete item group Delete group __ROOT__, GroupDescription, and GroupBusinessPolicies.
    Create SSKU listing draft Insert root and ItemDraft; insert ItemDraftCompatibility only when compatibility exists.
    Create MSKU listing draft Insert root, ItemGroupDraft, ItemGroupDraftDesc, optional ItemGroupDraftCompatibility, and one ItemDraft per variation.
    Update listing draft Batch upsert root and current item documents, delete removed variation items, upsert/delete the applicable compatibility docset, and upsert/delete group/group-description documents as the draft changes between MSKU and SSKU.
    Delete listing draft Delete root, group/group-description/group-compatibility, item compatibility, and all descendant ItemDraft documents found by ancestor query.
    Create/update active user draft Insert, update, or upsert one UserItemDraftMap document.
    Delete active user drafts Delete the selected UserItemDraftMap documents.
    Insert revision history Insert one ListingRevision plus zero or more ListingRevisionDetail and VariationRevisionDetail children. Bulk backfill uses the same three docsets in one accumulated request.
    Revision expired-date maintenance Upsert the revision-history __ROOT__ document when that maintenance path is invoked.
    Archive hold update ArchiveItemProcessor inherits item update logic but overrides the hold-update keyspace; the proven archive write is ItemKeyspaceArchive/__ROOT__.
    Archive group version update ArchiveItemGroupProcessor overrides the group-version keyspace; the proven archive write is ItemGroupKeyspaceArchive/__ROOT__.

    4. V1 dual-write and feature-gated behavior

    V1 listing operations do not have a second, independent NuData schema. They flow through DualWriteProcessor, which dispatches operations such as add, relist, revise inventory, end, finalize, description, shipping, media, markdown-sale, second-chance, hold, and HTTPS updates to the V2 item/group processors. The resulting docsets are therefore the same item and group families described above.

    DraftDualWriteProcessor similarly converts V1 drafts into V2 ListingDraft content and can maintain the active UserItemDraftMap index. These writes may be asynchronous fire-and-forget tasks, and reconciliation or failure events can cause retries or alternate paths.

    Whether a V1 request writes NuData depends on runtime configuration and ramps. The code contains controls for disabling NuData, V2-primary migration, parallel update, create/update/end dual-write, and draft dual-write behavior. Consequently, the matrix describes code-proven targets, not a guarantee that every production request reaches every target.

    5. Counting methodology and caveats

    Counts

    Count type Result Meaning
    Declared logical docsets 22 Distinct docset classes/collection names represented in the live schema packages. Nested Java value objects are excluded.
    Normal item create/replace targets 6 The six item documents built by CoreItemProcessor for the standard payload path.
    Normal item-group create/replace targets 3 Group root, group description, and group business policies.
    Listing-draft logical targets 6 Six declared draft docsets; one SSKU request writes root plus item and optional compatibility (2–3 documents), while an MSKU request writes root, group, description, optional compatibility, and one item document per variation (3+N or 4+N documents).
    User active-draft target 1 UserItemDraftMap.
    Normal revision insert targets 1–3 Revision plus optional listing and variation detail children; the revision root is not in this normal insert.
    Proven keyspace-qualified write sinks 24 The 22 live logical pairs plus two archive root pairs.

    Important boundaries

    • The counts are for NuData/MonStor structures visible in this repository. They exclude Oracle, Caty, SaleBO, and any downstream physical stores.
    • A docset count is not a row/document count. MSKU drafts and revisions can create many documents in one logical docset.
    • A class in the package is not proof that every path writes it. For example, revision __ROOT__ is declared and read but omitted from the ordinary revision insert; UuidMap and ItemLock are auxiliary flows.
    • Archive processors mostly read archive keyspaces. Only the overridden hold and group-version methods prove archive writes in this repository.
    • Deletes are key-specific. A delete request may remove a known set of child keys and may use an ancestor query for variation documents; it should not be interpreted as a schema drop or a physical table drop.
    • The document classes expose JSON property names, but this repository does not show a complete MonStor index/partition/replication DDL. The inventory therefore describes document shape and key ancestry, not every physical storage-engine index.
    • Dual-write order, async execution, retry, rollback, and ramps affect when a write is observed and whether it is attempted. They do not create additional logical NuData docsets.

    6. Q&A for Item and ItemGroup readers

    This section answers questions a reader familiar with eBay shopping concepts may ask when first encountering the Java entities and NuData docsets. The answers are limited to behavior shown by this repository.

    What is an Item in this service?

    Item is the Java V2 entity for one listing-level or variation-level sellable record. For an SSKU listing, it represents the single sellable item. For an MSKU listing, the service creates one Item for each active variation, with variation-specific quantity, price, identifiers, and other item state.

    What is an ItemGroup?

    ItemGroup is the Java V2 entity for the shared parent of an MSKU listing. It stores the group identity, title, membership, variation traits, shared images, group description, and group-level business policies. It is not a second copy of every variation's commercial state.

    Does every Item have an ItemGroup?

    No. An SSKU item has an ItemKeyspace record family and no corresponding ItemGroupKeyspace record. An MSKU item has an ItemGroupKeyspace group, and its item root contains grpId pointing to that group.

    If an MSKU has only one active variation, is the ItemGroup still created?

    Yes. The repository uses the MSKU classification flag, not the number of active variations, to decide whether a group exists. An MSKU with one active variation has one item record and an item-group root whose itemIds list contains exactly that one variation item ID.

    Is a one-item itemIds list evidence that the listing is SSKU?

    No. A one-entry itemIds list can be a valid one-variation MSKU group. To classify the listing, use the MSKU/SSKU behavior and the presence of the ItemGroupKeyspace group, not the list length alone.

    Is the eBay seller SKU the same as the NuData item ID?

    No. The NuData item ID (Item.itemId, persisted as PK) identifies the service record. The seller-provided SKU is represented separately by Item.sellerItemKey and persisted as slrItemKey. For a group, the seller's group key is stored on ItemGroup.sellerGroupKey.

    Does one Java Item object map to one NuData document?

    No. The Java Item is converted into an item document family. The root ItemKeyspace/__ROOT__ contains broad item state, while child docsets hold compatibility, description, quantity/price, details, and business-policy state. A full create/replace can therefore write six item docsets for one Java Item.

    Does one Java ItemGroup object map to one NuData document?

    No. A full group write creates or upserts three documents under the group ancestor: __ROOT__, GroupDescription, and GroupBusinessPolicies. ItemGroup is the source entity; the docsets are the persistence representation.

    Are ItemKeyspace and ItemGroupKeyspace SQL tables?

    They are logical NuData/MonStor keyspace families, not SQL tables shown by this repository. Each docset class defines a persisted document shape and each ancestor/child key defines where that document belongs. The counts in this document are logical docset and proven sink counts, not relational table DDL counts.

    Which fields belong to the item and which belong to the group?

    Variation-specific commercial state belongs to Item, especially quantity and price in ItemQtyPrice, along with variation identifiers and lifecycle state. Shared MSKU state belongs to ItemGroup, including membership, variation traits, group description, shared images, and group policies. The converter explicitly clears description, business policies, compatibilities, and global identifiers from MSKU variation Item objects when those values are stored at group level.

    Where is the description stored for SSKU versus MSKU?

    For SSKU, the description is written to the item's ItemDescription docset. For MSKU, the shared description is written to the group's GroupDescription docset rather than repeated on every variation item.

    Where are business policies stored for SSKU versus MSKU?

    For SSKU, return, payment, and shipping terms are written to the item's BusinessPolicies docset and related root policy fields. For MSKU, the shared terms are written to GroupBusinessPolicies; the group collection name is different even though the policy field schema is inherited from the item business-policy model.

    Where are price and quantity stored?

    Price and quantity are variation/item-level state. They are primarily written to ItemQtyPrice, whose price object contains fixed, auction, marketing, original-retail, cost-of-goods, and price-guarantee values, while availQty, qtySold, and minRmntSet hold inventory-related values. They are not represented by the group root merely because the item belongs to an MSKU group.

    Are Price, Lifecycle, and BusinessPolicies separate docsets?

    No. In the item root, Price and Lifecycle are embedded Java value objects. The policy terms inside the item root are also embedded. BusinessPolicies is a separate docset only when referring to the item policy document; the group equivalent is the separate GroupBusinessPolicies docset.

    Why do several keyspaces have a __ROOT__ class?

    The class name is reused across different Java packages, but the schemas and collections are different. ItemKeyspace/__ROOT__ is an item root, ItemGroupKeyspace/__ROOT__ is a group root, and the draft and revision roots have their own meanings. They must be identified by keyspace and docset, not by the class name alone.

    Can a consumer reconstruct a complete MSKU listing from the group alone?

    No. The group provides shared structure and the list of variation item IDs. The consumer must fetch the corresponding ItemKeyspace item families to obtain each variation's price, quantity, lifecycle, and other item-level state. Conversely, an item alone does not establish the complete group membership.
